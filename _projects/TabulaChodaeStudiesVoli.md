## Description

Tabula chordae studies documents the earliest explorations of a self-built string instrument: a hollow-bodied, twin-neck table steel developed as both instrument and process. Conceived in response to a growing disenchantment with the familiarity of the electric guitar, the instrument was designed to remain close enough to feel instinctive while introducing new physical and musical possibilities. The name Tabula Chordae was chosen - a Latin-inspired phrase intended to evoke a "surface of strings". Adopted to distinguish it from the traditions of both the electric guitar and the conventional lap steel.

The project began not only as an exploration of sound, but also as an exploration of instrument making. Designing and building the instrument became an integral part of the creative process, offering opportunities to experiment with ideas, construction methods and playing techniques that would have been difficult to realise within the constraints of an existing instrument. Rather than representing a finished design, Tabula Chordae is intended as an evolving instrument whose construction and capabilities will continue to develop over time.

Rather than beginning with established techniques or predetermined compositional methods, these recordings were created through experimentation and sustained interaction with an unfamiliar instrument. Its physical constraints, resonances, imperfections and unexpected behaviours shaped the direction of the music as much as conscious musical decisions.

Across the album, slow ambient passages sit alongside distorted loops, freeform improvisations, unstable textures, repetition, noise and moments of more direct melodic playing. These contrasts are not presented as opposing approaches, but as different methods of investigation: attempts to understand the instrument through resonance, feedback, gesture, repetition, friction and failure.

The term Studies is used deliberately. These recordings are not intended as definitive compositions or demonstrations of mastery, but as documents of development. They capture the earliest stages of learning a new instrument, when uncertainty, curiosity and experimentation remain central to the creative process.

As a result, the performances remain intentionally close to their original form. Small mistakes, uneven gestures, hesitations, abandoned ideas and imperfect takes remain throughout the recordings. Rather than being corrected or concealed, they are preserved as evidence of exploration: traces of learning, adaptation and the gradual development of a personal performance language.

Tabula chordae studies occupies the space between instrument design and musical composition. It documents the first chapter in the life of Tabula Chordae: an instrument that continues to evolve alongside the music created with it
